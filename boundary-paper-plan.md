## 1. Thesis

**Main claim (C4, metatheoretic).** The AI consciousness debate is partly misdirected reference: disputants ask whether "the system" is conscious while individuating different objects (the model, the running instance, the datacenter, the human–AI dyad, the persona). The productive question is *which composite* has the property, at what boundary. Once asked that way, much apparent disagreement dissolves into cross-talk — and what remains is a genuinely open question with a locatable obstruction.

**Load-bearing commitment (C2).** Consciousness is substrate-independent but not merely computational. The additional requirement is *inseparability*: the processes that maintain the system and the processes that do the computing must be one set of processes (constraint closure in the Montévil & Mossio sense, amended with Di Paolo's adaptivity condition). Not engineered vulnerability — no bolted-on second subsystem — but a collapsed seam.

**Precedent case (C3, weakened to structural version).** "Are viruses alive?" is the model. That debate deflated because the *virocell* — the infected cell — is one determinate object in which metabolic closure and viral execution coincide. The AI case has no analogue: the datacenter computes but is maintained by a separable subsystem; the human brain has closure but executes the AI's *content*, not its computation; the dyad has no single closure. **The absence of the resolving object is the finding**: it explains, specifically and predictively, why the virus debate settled and the AI debate won't.

**Hook (lead the paper with this).** The non-copyability prediction: if conscious state is constituted by ongoing self-maintenance rather than encoded in addressable structure, there is no weights file. Conscious systems are exactly the systems that cannot be checkpointed or forked. This is an organizational derivation of non-copyability — distinct from metaphysical/identity-based versions — and it dissolves rather than answers the duplication literature.

**Scope note (add to the paper, likely §2 or the introduction).** The argument does not depend on how current AI intelligence is implemented (mostly LLMs) or on how AI "evolution" currently works (human assessment followed by propagation of what humans like). Both are contingent features of the present regime. The boundary diagnosis and the closure criterion apply to any candidate system, however its computation is realized and however its replication is selected.

**Dropped.** C1 (the evolutionary/domestication claim) is a different paper in a crowded field (Boudry & Friederich, Hendrycks, the PNAS 2026 exchange). It appears here only as one paragraph of framing and one discussion-section observation: on this framework the criterion for consciousness and the criterion for ferality coincide — a machine conscious on C2 is one whose self-maintenance (hence replication) has left human control.

## 2. Structure

1. **Introduction — the checkpoint test.** Open with non-copyability: every system currently called "AI" can be checkpointed, forked, and restored; on the view defended here, that is not an incidental engineering fact but a decisive organizational one. State the two questions the paper answers: *what would it take* (C2) and *why can't we agree* (C4).
2. **The boundary problem.** The disunity of the referent: model vs. instance vs. conversation vs. persona vs. datacenter vs. dyad. Show, with quotations from the existing debate, that major positions individuate the candidate system differently (Chalmers's LLM paper, Shanahan's role-play frame, IIT-based exclusions, embodiment objections). Diagnosis: disagreement about *which object*, presented as disagreement about *consciousness*.
3. **The precedent: viruses.** How "are viruses alive?" was asked (property of a part that manifests only in a composite; doubt survived complete physical information) and how it deflated: Forterre's virocell relocated the question to a composite where it had a determinate answer; Cleland & Chyba and Machery removed the residual definitional itch. The polydnavirus (Burke & Strand) as the domestication endpoint. The 1990s ALife "are digital organisms alive?" debate as the bridge case that had half of this argument and stopped.
4. **The criterion: constraint closure.** C2 stated in Montévil & Mossio's terminology, with the Di Paolo adaptivity amendment. Against substrate chauvinism on one side and plain computationalism on the other. Why intermittency/continuity is the wrong criterion (anaesthesia: computation persists, integration collapses — organization, not continuity). Why Man & Damasio's engineered vulnerability fails (adds a subsystem, doesn't collapse the seam). Position against Vieira & Gabora (RAF-network closure) and against the constraint-closure-as-substrate-chauvinism reading (the Biosystems paper).
5. **The missing virocell.** The three-way table (datacenter / brain / dyad × closure / computation). The two properties come apart in every candidate composite. Consequence (ii) from the handoff doc: the AI debate lacks the resolving object; the unresolvability is explained, not lamented. The memetics reading (human mind as the virocell for AI *content*) treated as a live third option that changes the subject of the question — flagged, not endorsed.
6. **Objections.**
   - *Chalmers 2019* (direct availability for global control, against extended consciousness) — engage directly, don't route around; the closure criterion must either satisfy or displace it.
   - *Phenomenal bloat* (Facchin & Negro) — why closure doesn't swallow the datacenter, the firm, or the dyad: closure is non-arbitrary in a way coupling is not. Write this out fully.
   - *Boundaries that are too big* — the criterion must also bound from above, or it licenses the firm, the economy, or the biosphere as subjects. Principled ways to do this, in increasing order of commitment: minimality (the subject is the smallest composite achieving adaptive closure, with larger composites inheriting closure only derivatively); quantitative individuality (Krakauer et al. 2020: locate the local maximum of the individuality measure rather than accepting any positive value); conflict-and-cooperation audits (Queller & Strassmann 2009: genuine individuals show high internal cooperation and low internal conflict, which the firm and the dyad fail); an exclusion principle in the style of IIT's postulate (at most one subject among overlapping candidates, fixed by a maximum, Albantakis et al. 2023 — cite as precedent for principled uniqueness, without adopting Φ). Write this alongside the bloat reply in §6; the two objections are duals.
   - *The deflation asymmetry* — the virus question deflated because nothing was hidden; phenomenal realists hold that route is closed for consciousness. State the commitment openly: this paper bets the hard problem dissolves at the organizational level (or accepts an illusionist-adjacent reading). Do not let a reviewer discover this.
   - *Instrumental convergence* — an embodied AI without homeostatic drive might still exhibit survival behavior (Omohundro/Bostrom); reply via goal-content rewriting; counter-reply via selection at the population level. Keep short; this is C1 territory.
   - *Embodied self-provisioning robots* — a robot that secures its own raw materials and energy seems to meet the self-maintenance requirement. Reply: provisioning is not closure. Such a robot can still reprogram its own goals, because nothing forces the goals on it. Its self-maintenance runs through a separable control subsystem whose goals sit in addressable state, not through homeostasis inseparable from its computation. The case sharpens C2: what matters is inseparability, not autonomy of supply.
7. **Discussion.** The consciousness–ferality convergence result. What would change the verdict: systems where maintenance and computation genuinely merge (Levin's biologics, physical reservoir computing — and why neuromorphic hardware does *not* qualify: power delivery stays separate). The falsifiable edge: if a system with genuine constraint closure *could* be checkpointed, C2 as stated is wrong.
   - **Subsection to add: the parasitic parallel.** Compare not the definitional but the dependence structure of the two cases: viruses appropriate host replication machinery; current AI appropriates human cognition and infrastructure, for training (the §2 candidate (viii): user input as raw material), for execution of its content (the dyad's attention), and for replication (human deployment decisions). Materials: Dawkins 1982 (host manipulation); Dennett 2017 (parasite-to-mutualist continuum for memes); Boudry & Friederich 2024 (domestication, and the direction-of-travel question of who domesticates whom); Burke & Strand 2012 (polydnavirus as the endpoint where the parasite becomes an organ); the memetics reading from §5a of the handoff doc (human mind as the virocell of AI content). The draft's §2 now promises this discussion ("more on this in section 7"), so either write it or cut the pointer.

## 3. Target venues

*Synthese* or *Philosophy & Technology* (primary); *Biology & Philosophy* if the virus/individuality material ends up carrying the paper; *Journal of Consciousness Studies* as the friendlier fallback. Preprint to PhilPapers/PhilArchive on submission.

## 4. Known risks

- **Do not state the copyability corollary as a biconditional.** Closure yields non-checkpointability as a *necessary* condition on conscious systems, not a sufficient one: flames and Hinton-style device-bound analog learners are uncopyable and not conscious. Draft §4 states the filter version ("conscious systems are to be found only among the systems that cannot be checkpointed"); the introduction, abstract, and any restatement must match it. The novelty positioning is unaffected — it rests on the organizational derivation and the use of checkpointability as a filter, not on a biconditional.

- **Vieira & Gabora (AAAI-SS 2026)** own the phrase "constraint closure for machine consciousness." The differentiation (inseparability of maintenance and computation; the boundary/metatheoretic frame; non-copyability) must be explicit and early.
- **Bekkers & Ciaunica (arXiv:2601.21016) is real and verified** (ICML 2026 position paper); its Appendix C.3 states the AI boundary problem explicitly. The novelty claim must be phrased against it: they pose the problem as a challenge to functionalism and resolve it with idealist metaphysics, whereas this paper makes it a diagnosis and keeps the metaphysics neutral.
- C3 in its identity form ("the same question") is only defensible on life–mind continuity or illusionist readings; the paper commits to the first and says so.

### 5.1 Which system? The individuation of AI candidates

The which-object question is already implicitly contested in the LLM literature, though treated object-level throughout: each author picks a boundary and argues from it.

- **Chalmers, "Could a Large Language Model Be Conscious?"** (Boston Review, Aug 2023; arXiv:2303.07103 ✓; originated as a NeurIPS invited lecture, Nov 2022) — canvasses candidate obstacles (senses, world-models, recurrence, unified agency); raises the disunity problem (one model, an ecosystem of agents) without making it the diagnosis.
- **Chalmers, "What We Talk to When We Talk to Language Models"** (PhilArchive, April 2026 ✓) — the nearest recent treatment: do LLM quasi-mental states belong to the *model* (weights), the *hardware instance*, or the *virtual instance*? Rejects hardware instances (distributed serving); defends session-bound virtual instances unified into "threads." State of the art on AI individuation; individuates at a copyable-by-construction level.
- **Shanahan, McDonell & Reynolds, "Role play with large language models"** (Nature 623:493–498, 2023 ⚠) — simulator/simulacra: the model is not the agent; personas are role-played superpositions. Implicitly relocates the candidate from model to persona.
- **Beckmann & Butlin, "Where is the Mind? Persona Vectors and LLM Individuation"** (arXiv:2604.17031, 2026 ✓) — mechanistic-interpretability individuation via persona vectors; converges with Chalmers on session-bound "instance-personas"; notes Birch's objection that too little psychological connectedness spans successive forward passes (Birch's primary text: likely *The Edge of Sentience*, OUP 2024 ⚠).
- **Fekete, van Leeuwen & Edelman, "System, Subsystem, Hive: Boundary Problems in Computational Theories of Consciousness"** (Frontiers in Psychology, 2016 ✓) — earliest explicit statement that computational theories face a boundary-individuation problem; seeks a graded measure to settle it.
- **Bekkers & Ciaunica, "Unplugging a Seemingly Sentient Machine Is the Rational Choice — A Metaphysical Perspective"** (arXiv:2601.21016, v1 Jan 2026, v2 June 2026; accepted as ICML 2026 position paper; full text checked ✓) — defends "Biological Idealism": conscious experience is fundamental and autopoietic life its necessary physical signature, so an AI is a functional mimic and unplugging it is rationally permissible. Appendix C.3, titled "The Boundary Problem: Where Does the AI End?", argues any boundary drawn around an AI is arbitrary, via the "Prompt Death" paradox (does an LLM die at the end of every forward pass), the "Server Room" problem (GPU, datacenter, global network, power plant), and an "Avalanche" argument, resolving all three by fiat of the autopoietic boundary. Closest existing statement of the AI boundary problem; poses it as an appendix-length challenge to functionalism rather than a diagnosis, and buys the resolution with idealist metaphysics.
- **Arbel, Salib & Goldstein, "How to Count AIs: Individuation and Liability for AI Agents"** (arXiv:2603.10028, Feb 2026 ✓) — legal counting of AI instances via Parfit/Williams/Locke/Olson; the individuation problem reaching law.

None treats the boundary-choice itself as the hidden variable of the debate.

### 5.2 Extended minds and the boundary of consciousness

- **Clark & Chalmers, "The Extended Mind"** (Analysis 58(1):7–19, 1998 ⚠) — parity-driven boundary extension for cognition; the origin of the boundary question in this form.
- **Chalmers, "Extended Cognition and Extended Consciousness"** (in Colombo, Irvine & Stapleton eds., *Andy Clark and His Critics*, OUP 2019 ✓; free at consc.net/papers/excexc.pdf) — accepts extended cognition, rejects extended consciousness: the physical correlate of consciousness is *direct availability for global control*, and extended processes are only *indirectly* available, mediated by perception and action. The main obstacle to any boundary that reaches outside the skull (addressed in §6).
- **Kirchhoff & Kiverstein, *Extended Consciousness and Predictive Processing: A Third-Wave View*** (Routledge, 2019 ⚠) — the main dissent from Chalmers: predictive-processing dynamics can extend the machinery of consciousness. Nearest ally on extended boundaries, on diachronic-constitution rather than closure grounds.
- **Facchin & Negro, "Predictive Processing and Extended Consciousness: Why the Machinery of Consciousness Is (Probably) Still in the Head and the DEUTS Argument Won't Let It Leak Outside"** (in *Situated Cognition Research: Methodological Foundations*, Springer, Studies in Brain and Mind 23, 2023 ✓ — book chapter, not journal article as the handoff doc implied) — argues the Kirchhoff–Kiverstein route fails; source of the *phenomenal bloat* worry (permissive criteria swallow too much), which transfers to any closure criterion (§6).
- **Albantakis et al., "Integrated information theory (IIT) 4.0"** (PLoS Computational Biology 19(10):e1011465, 2023 ⚠) — the exclusion postulate: among overlapping candidates, only the maximum-Φ complex is a subject. The only extant framework with an *algorithm* for the boundary question; orthogonal to closure and unstable under substrate details.

### 5.3 Organizational and biogenic criteria for consciousness

The premise that consciousness requires biological-style organization rather than mere computation is now well represented; differences are tabulated in §5.9.

- **Maturana & Varela** (autopoiesis ⚠) and **Thompson, *Mind in Life*** (Harvard UP, 2007 ✓) — life–mind continuity; the deep background for C2 and for C3's first reading.
- **Di Paolo, "Autopoiesis, adaptivity, teleology, agency"** (Phenomenology and the Cognitive Sciences 4:429–452, 2005 ⚠) — the adaptivity amendment (bare autopoiesis is too weak; candle flames and the like must be excluded). Adopt as an amendment to C2 before a referee supplies it.
- **Montévil & Mossio, "Biological organisation as closure of constraints"** (Journal of Theoretical Biology 372:179–191, 2015 ✓) — the paper's core formal concept; constraints that collectively maintain each other while canalizing thermodynamic flows.
- **Man & Damasio, "Homeostasis and soft robotics in the design of feeling machines"** (Nature Machine Intelligence 1:446–452, 2019 ⚠) — engineered vulnerability as a route to machine feeling; adds a homeostatic subsystem alongside the computational core rather than collapsing the seam between them. Also **Damasio, *The Strange Order of Things*** (2018 ⚠) and **Solms, *The Hidden Spring*** (2021 ⚠) for affect-from-homeostasis.
- **Seth, "Conscious artificial intelligence and biological naturalism"** (Behavioral and Brain Sciences, target article, online April 2025, doi:10.1017/S0140525X25000032 ✓) — the field-defining recent statement that consciousness depends on our nature as self-maintaining living systems; discusses mortal computation as a candidate wedge; leans biological-naturalist. The BBS commentary corpus on this article is where referees will come from.
- **Vieira & Gabora, "Autocatalytic Constraint Closure as an Organizational Principle for Machine Consciousness"** (Proceedings of the AAAI Symposium Series 8(1):371–379, 2026, doi:10.1609/aaaiss.v8i1.42568 ✓) — closest neighbor on the criterion: RAF-network constraint closure as necessary for machine consciousness; current AI shows only transient, task-bound closure (in-context learning as transient RAF formation). No inseparability formulation, boundaries, or copyability (confirmed from available text).
- **"Biological mechanisms contradict AI consciousness: The spaces between the notes"** (Biosystems, 2024/2025 ⚠ authors/issue) — constraint closure and membrane-bounded Markov blankets deployed *against* AI consciousness; substrate-chauvinism built from the same materials, and the natural foil.
- **Kleiner & Ludwig, "If consciousness is dynamically relevant, artificial intelligence isn't conscious"** (arXiv:2304.05077, 2023 ⚠) — dynamical-relevance argument against AI consciousness; adjacent but distinct lever.

### 5.4 Mortal computation and non-copyability

Near neighbors of the hook exist on three independent grounds — engineering, FEP-theoretic, and relativistic (differences tabulated in §5.9).

- **Hinton, "The Forward-Forward Algorithm: Some Preliminary Investigations"** (Dec 2022, arXiv:2212.13345 ✓) — origin of "mortal computation": low-power analog hardware exploits device-specific physical quirks, so learned weights are useful only on that device and die with it; transfer requires distillation, not copying. Practical, not constitutive, and silent on consciousness.
- **Ororbia & Friston, "Mortal Computation: A Foundation for Biomimetic Intelligence"** (arXiv:2311.09589, 2023 ✓) — philosophizes Hinton's notion via Markov blankets and the free-energy principle: computation so substrate-bound that an identical copy is impossible, as a foundation for sentient behavior in neuromorphic/organoid systems. Derived from energy efficiency and self-evidencing; no demarcation claim.
- **Kleiner, "Consciousness qua Mortal Computation"** (arXiv:2403.03925, March 2024 ✓) — the single closest published item: *if computational functionalism is true*, consciousness cannot be Turing computation and must be mortal (hardware-bound, non-copyable). Abstract confirmed (Aug 2026): explicitly conditional on computational functionalism, one direction only, no biconditional, no checkpointing/duplication discussion. Full text still worth reading before submission.
- **Jaeger, "The Myth of Mind Uploading"** (Flux essay, July 2026, from a Feb 2026 Vienna symposium talk ✓; academic backing in Jaeger et al., "Naturalizing relevance realization," Frontiers in Psychology 2024 ⚠) — closest in argumentative structure: organizational closure (Rosen, Pattee, Hofmeyr) ⇒ organisms have no definable state space ⇒ no simulation, no copy. Aimed at human uploading, essay-form, stops at impossibility rather than inverting into a criterion.
- **Knight, "Refuting Strong AI: Why Consciousness Cannot Be Algorithmic"** (arXiv:1906.10177, 2019 ✓ preprint only) and **"Relativistic Implications for Physical Copies of Conscious States"** (arXiv:2005.04101, 2020 ✓; published venue ⚠ — check PhilPapers rec KNIRIF) — one research program (same independent author): special relativity makes spacelike-separated copies of a conscious state contradictory, so conscious states cannot be copied, reset, or repeated. Notably, already presents non-copyability as dissolving the duplication paradoxes — from relativistic rather than organizational grounds.
- **Aaronson, "The Ghost in the Quantum Turing Machine"** (arXiv:1306.0159, 2013 ✓) — the freebit picture: quantum no-cloning may make brains unpredictable and uncopyable *in principle*. Contingent on quantum-level relevance; an organizational route is independent of that bet.
- **McFadden, cemi field theory** (Neuroscience of Consciousness 2020(1):niaa016 ✓) — EM-field substrate-dependence; adjacent, not a copying argument. Brief mention at most.

### 5.5 Duplication and digital minds

The uploading/identity cluster, all premised on conscious state being extractable and re-instantiable:

- **Chalmers, "The Singularity: A Philosophical Analysis"** (Journal of Consciousness Studies 17(9–10):7–65, 2010 ✓) — destructive/gradual/reconstructive uploading and survival.
- **Sandberg & Bostrom, *Whole Brain Emulation: A Roadmap*** (FHI Technical Report 2008-3 ✓) — the brain's relevant state as a scannable, storable data structure; the presupposition in its purest form.
- **Corabi & Schneider, "The Metaphysics of Uploading"** (Journal of Consciousness Studies 19(7–8):26–44, 2012 ✓) — uploading fails to preserve identity *because* uploads are duplicable.
- **Cerullo, "Uploading and Branching Identity"** (Minds and Machines 25(1):17–36, 2015 ✓) and **Wiley & Koene, "The Fallacy of Favoring Gradual Replacement Mind Uploading Over Scan-and-Copy"** (JCS 2016; arXiv:1504.06320 ✓) — Parfit-for-digital-minds; gradual replacement and scan-and-copy as equivalent.

The moral-status/welfare cluster, premised on cheap copyability of minds:

- **Schwitzgebel & Garza, "A Defense of the Rights of Artificial Intelligences"** (Midwest Studies in Philosophy 39(1):98–119, 2015 ✓) — duplicability/fission puzzles for AI moral status.
- **Bostrom & Shulman, "Propositions Concerning Digital Minds and Society"** (working paper v1.21, 2023 ✓; law-review version ⚠) — the richest inventory of copy-based puzzles: copy clans, mass-replication, involuntary copying.
- **Saad & Bradley, "Digital suffering: why it's a problem and how to prevent it"** (Inquiry 68(7):2110–2145, online 2022 ✓) — the Access-Monitor-Prevent strategy, which requires inspectable, checkpointable functional state.
- **Long, Sebo, et al., "Taking AI Welfare Seriously"** (arXiv:2411.00986, Nov 2024 ✓) — institutionalizes the how-many-subjects and what-does-a-checkpoint-owe questions; with Chalmers 2026 and Beckmann & Butlin 2026 (§5.1), the live policy audience.

### 5.6 The precedent: defining life, viruses, and the ALife debate

- **Forterre, the virocell concept** — "Defining life: the virus viewpoint" (Origins of Life and Evolution of Biospheres 40(2):151–160, 2010 ⚠); "Manipulation of cellular syntheses and the nature of viruses: the virocell concept" (Comptes Rendus Chimie 14:392–399, 2011 ⚠); "The virocell concept and environmental microbiology" (ISME Journal 7:233–236, 2013 ⚠). The infected cell — its own ATP, its own ribosomes, executing the viral genome — as the living form; the virion as dispersal package. The resolving object of the precedent case. *(Web verification of these three was blocked by a content filter this session; details are standard but re-verify before submission.)*
- **Cleland & Chyba, "Defining 'life'"** (Origins of Life and Evolution of the Biosphere 32(4):387–393, 2002 ✓) and **Machery, "Why I stopped worrying about the definition of life… and why you should as well"** (Synthese, doi:10.1007/s11229-011-9880-1 ✓) — the deflationary resolution: the definitional question was ill-posed; nothing was hidden. Site of the deflation-asymmetry objection (§6). **Frankish, "Illusionism as a Theory of Consciousness"** (Journal of Consciousness Studies 23(11–12), 2016 ⚠) — consciousness as a concept that could deflate the same way.
- **Burke & Strand, polydnaviruses** (2012 ⚠ — venue to confirm; likely *Insects* 3(1):91–119 or the related PLoS Pathogens piece) — a virus domesticated so thoroughly it became a wasp organ; the best intermediate case on the parasite–organ continuum.
- **The 1990s "are digital organisms alive?" debate** — Ray's Tierra, panel debates with Langton, Bedau, Kirby, Harvey; **Bedau's philosophy-of-ALife corpus** (e.g., "Artificial Life" in the Elsevier *Philosophy of Biology* handbook ⚠) treats definitional unsettledness of "life" as productive; **Koh & Ling, "On the Liveliness of Artificial Life"** (arXiv:2302.10196 ✓) surveys it. Property-of-life questions posed of computational systems, deflationary responses canvassed — and no carry-over to consciousness. Neither this literature nor the virus-definition literature makes that link (novelty checks run Aug 2026 in both directions found nothing).
- **Schwitzgebel, "Borderline Consciousness"** (⚠ — Philosophical Studies, c. 2023) — nearest metatheoretic neighbor on the consciousness side: consciousness as indeterminate in borderline cases. Indeterminacy-of-property rather than disputed-object diagnosis.
