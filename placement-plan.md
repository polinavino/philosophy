## 0. The one-paragraph strategy

You are a formal-methods/CS academic writing analytic philosophy from outside the
discipline. That is **not** a barrier to publication: philosophy's dominant review model is
*double-anonymous*, and the strongest venues are *triple-anonymous* (author hidden from the
handling editor too), which neutralises affiliation bias even at the desk-reject stage. The
real bar is (1) **scope fit** — pick the journal by what it publishes, and (2) **engaging the
existing literature in its own terms** — the single most common reason outsider papers are
desk-rejected. The per-paper "must-cite / scoop risk" sections below are your inoculation
against (2). List yourself as **Independent Researcher** or your actual CS department; never
invent a philosophy affiliation. Deposit everything on **PhilArchive** (no affiliation gate,
unlike arXiv) and index on **PhilPapers** for discoverability. Realistic target per paper is
**R&R, not acceptance**, over ~2 years and multiple rounds; submit to one journal at a time.

## 2. logic-morality — *Logical Structure as Memetic Fitness*

**Status: ready.** This is the flagship. A non-cognitivist argument that the logical structure
of moral systems (consistency, universalisability, overridingness) is a *memetic-fitness /
transmission* feature, not a normative requirement, integrating Sperber's epidemiology + Dunbar's
scaling, with a novel "Frege-Geach as feature" reading.

anonymized PDF). Note: Ergo charges a **$20 submission fee**, waivable on request if you can't
afford it; publication itself is free. Author guidelines: https://quod.lib.umich.edu/e/ergo/submit/

### Framing caution
The Sperber tradition (Sperber, Scott-Phillips, Claidière, Heintz) **rejects** Dawkinsian
replication in favour of *attraction*. A paper that says "memetic fitness" while leaning on Sperber
must pre-empt the attraction-vs-selection objection — turn that tension into a strength rather than
walk into it. Cite the 2018 Cultural Attraction Theory synthesis (Scott-Phillips, Blancke, Heintz),
not just Sperber 1996.

## 8. metaphysics — *Humean Structural Realism*

**Status: handoff notes**, explicitly written to seed a future draft, not a paper.

**The framing that makes it publishable (this is the key strategic finding):** "Humean structural
realism" as a *named position* is essentially **unoccupied territory**. Build the paper around a
matrix that distinguishes your cell — *structuralist ontology (world IS the structure) + Humean/BSA
laws-as-compressions + anti-plenitude* — from each neighbour:
- **Tegmark (MUH):** structure-identity but *full plenitude* → your anti-plenitude + the noise fork differentiate.
- **Ladyman & Ross (OSR):** structure but *primitive modality* → you keep structuralism, go Humean instead.
- **Lewis:** concrete worlds, not identified with math structures.
- **Maudlin:** primitive laws. **Jill North:** structure as guide to ontology, agnostic on Humeanism.

Occupying an empty cell is your **best defence as an outsider** — you're naming new territory, not
re-litigating a crowded debate.

**Read first / must-cite:** **Michael Esfeld** (Lausanne, "Super-Humeanism" — your nearest occupied
territory and sharpest likely critic) and **Eddy Keming Chen (2025), "The Simplicity of Physical Laws"**
(*Noûs* — already frames your open fork: why our block is law-like rather than noise). Also **Jill North,
*Physics, Structure, and Reality*** (2021), **Loewer** (BSA), **Nina Emery** (the anti-Humean you must answer),
**Bricker, "Plenitude of Possible Structures"** (the anti-plenitude target).

**Homes:** *BJPS* (triple-anon, ≤10k words — if you can make it decisive and tight), *Philosophers'
Imprint* or *Synthese* (if it needs 13–18k for the full differentiation), *European Journal for
Philosophy of Science*, *Erkenntnis*, *Ergo*. (Note: there is no longer a standalone Elsevier
phil-of-physics journal — Part B was merged into *Studies in History and Philosophy of Science*.)
