Dear Editors,

I am submitting "Logical Structure as Memetic Fitness: A Non-Cognitivist Account of Moral
Consistency" for consideration in Ergo.

The paper argues that the logical structure typically demanded of moral systems (consistency,
universalisability, overridingness) is best understood not as a normative requirement but as a
memetic-fitness property of moral systems that succeed in propagation. It integrates Sperber's
epidemiology of cultural representations with Dunbar's work on the scaling of human social groups
to identify the selection environment in which logical structure becomes fitness-decisive, and
offers a new reading of the Frege-Geach problem as a structural feature moral discourse exploits
for transmission rather than a defect non-cognitivism must repair.

The paper is approximately 11,800 words, is not under consideration elsewhere, and is prepared
for anonymous review. I am an independent researcher with a background in formal methods and
computer science, writing in metaethics from outside the discipline. I would be grateful for the
referees' guidance.

Thank you for your consideration.
