# Logic–Morality: revision notes & next steps

**Status:** Rejected at Ergo (Aug 2026), single referee report. Verdict is a *reject*, but an
encouraging one — "I encourage the author(s) to continue... this project could become a valuable
contribution." Read this as *develop substantially and resubmit elsewhere*, not R&R.

---

## 1. What the referee actually said

The report is short but its structure is clear. It grants the project ("a naturalistic etiological
account of why logical structure is something like a cross-culturally universal property of ethical
thinking — is exciting") and then makes **two** demands, one about each half of an etiological
explanation:

- **(A) The explanandum is thin and descriptively contentious.** "The sense in which 'moral
  systems' are 'logical' is very thinly sketched, and even that thin sketch is highly contentious as
  a descriptive account of our moral practices."
- **(B) The explanans is stitched-together controversial ideas, engaged without precision.** "The
  current etiological account appears largely to consist of stitching together a host of interesting
  but themselves controversial ideas (Haidt on dumbfounding, Sperber and Mercier on reasoning etc.)
  in a way that is hard to evaluate because these ideas are never engaged with precision and detail."

Closing mandate: "more precise development of both the explanandum and the explanans."

This is a deeper critique than the internal issues I flagged before submission (§6 below). It is
correct, and addressing it is a rebuild of the middle of the paper, not a polish. The good news is
that both demands are concrete and actionable.

## 2. Diagnosis: the structural risk the referee is circling

The paper's core mechanism — the §3.6 **stranger threshold** — currently rests on **two of the most
contested empirical claims in the cultural-evolution-of-religion literature at once**:

1. **Dunbar's number (~150)** as a real cognitive cap that defines the "personal acquaintance
   fails" boundary; and
2. **Norenzayan's Big Gods** causal story (moralizing high gods *enabled* large-scale cooperation).

Both are live, published disputes (see §4). A referee who knows the field sees the whole argument
resting on a single point of failure that is itself doubly contested. That is exactly the shape of
"controversial ideas engaged without detail." **The first strategic priority is to make the core
argument robust to the failure of any one borrowed empirical claim** — either by engaging each
controversy head-on and retreating to the minimal defensible version, or by re-grounding the
mechanism on the more robust parts of the literature (cultural group selection / scaling of
cooperation generally: Henrich, Richerson & Boyd) so it doesn't live or die by "150" and "Big Gods."

## 3. Part A — Sharpen the explanandum ("what is logical structure, and do moral practices have it?")

The paper defines logical structure as *consistency + universalisability + overridingness*, hedged
to "enough" of each (intro clarifications, l.83–98). Two problems the referee is pointing at:

- **It is under-specified.** "Enough apparent consistency to support inference" is not yet a
  characterization anyone could test or contest. Each of the three properties needs a precise
  statement: what exactly is consistency *for a preference-set that you also say can be locally
  inconsistent "without anything having gone wrong"* (l.103–104, l.390–393)? Right now the paper
  asserts convergence-on-consistency at the society level and permits inconsistency at the
  individual level, but never makes the society-level property precise enough to be the thing being
  explained. This tension is doing quiet damage and should be made explicit and resolved.
- **It is descriptively contested.** The claim that moral practice *has* this structure runs
  straight into the **anti-codifiability / particularist tradition**, which denies that moral
  thought is systematizable at all. This literature must be engaged directly, because it denies the
  explanandum outright:
  - Dancy, *Ethics Without Principles* (2004) — moral particularism; reasons are holistic, no
    principle-based structure.
  - McDowell, "Virtue and Reason" (1979); Wiggins — uncodifiability of the ethical.
  - Virtue ethics and casuistry as non-systematic, case-driven rivals.

**The equivocation to avoid (this is the crux of A).** It is tempting to think the explanandum is
obvious — "moral reasoning within a system is just applying logic to produce new moral conclusions."
That reply *confirms* the referee's objection; it does not answer it. Two things are being collapsed:

- **Local inference** — given conditional moral commitments, you can run modus ponens. Nearly
  trivial, and true. But inference is *available in inconsistent systems too* (ex falso: an
  inconsistent set entails everything), so "you can apply logic" is evidence-neutral on consistency
  and says nothing about universalisability or overridingness.
- **Global structural properties** — the whole *system* is consistent, universalisable, overriding.
  Substantive, contingent, contested. This is the real explanandum, and it is not entailed by local
  inference.

The vacuity horn is fatal, not safe: if "logical" just means "closed under a background logic," there
is nothing cross-culturally *contingent* for a selection story to explain, and the etiology
dissolves. The project needs the substantive reading. And "moral reasoning is just applying logic to
fixed premises" is itself **generalism/principlism** — exactly what particularism (Dancy) denies —
so asserting it as obvious is the contentious descriptive move, not a way around it. Note the paper
already concedes the tension: preferences "can be locally inconsistent without anything having gone
wrong" (l.103–104), which is coherent only once "supports inference" and "is globally consistent"
are separated and one is named as the target.

**The explosion-based derivation (a natural defence — steelman + the gaps it must pre-empt).** A
tempting way to *derive* rather than assume the explanandum: I adopt a system and reason with it; if
it is inconsistent I can derive everything (explosion) and must abandon it; if it has no logical
structure I cannot extend it to new cases and it does not propagate; so propagating systems have
logic. Right *kind* of move (deriving, not asserting), but three gaps to close:

- [ ] **Gap 1 — explosion assumes classical logic.** People in fact hold inconsistent moral views
  without deriving arbitrary conclusions or abandoning them; they quarantine contradictions
  (paraconsistent in practice) — which the paper itself concedes (l.103–104). Reframe the target as
  *enough consistency to stay action-guiding*, not global consistency, and explain contradiction-
  quarantining instead of invoking ex falso.
- [ ] **Gap 2 — "does not propagate" is a threshold; the paper's real claim is a gradient.** A
  memorized situation-list *can* propagate, just more expensively (§3.5). State it as a
  transmission-cost differential, not an on/off gate.
- [ ] **Gap 3 — the derivation yields consistency + generativity only.** It gives *nothing* on
  universalisability or overridingness. Give those two their own separate selection arguments, or
  stop presenting the derivation as grounding all three properties.
- [ ] **Level decision (blocks A1).** The explosion argument is first-person deliberative, but §3.4
  says selection runs on *transmission*, not private deliberation. Decide which argument is being
  run — a-priori "needs logic to reason," transmission-cost, or both-as-distinct — since it changes
  what A1 should claim. (Transmission-cost is the one that fits the rest of the paper.)

claim the argument needs, (c) show it survives the skeptical reading — or substitute something more
robust. The paper already does exactly this well for one pillar (the replicator-vs-attractor caveat
in §3.5); apply that standard everywhere.

## 5. Part C — Strategic decisions before rewriting

- **Scope.** The paper currently does a lot (non-cognitivism + memetics + Dunbar + value capture +
  Frege-Geach + law/morality + relativism + three future directions). The "stitching" impression is
  partly overreach. **Strongly consider narrowing:** develop the core etiology (Parts A + B) deeply,
  and cut or spin off the peripheral moves (value capture §3.8, law/morality §3.7, possibly
  Frege-Geach §4.1) into a second paper. Depth over breadth is the exact remedy the referee asked
  for.
- **Where next.** Given the interdisciplinary/naturalist framing, likely better-matched venues than
  Ergo: *Philosophical Psychology*, *Biology & Philosophy*, *Mind & Language*, *Journal of Moral
  Philosophy*, or *Philosophers' Imprint*. A naturalism-friendly venue will be more receptive to the
  method and more able to referee the empirical pillars fairly.
- **Regenerate the PDF** (`../build.sh`) only after revisions; the current PDF is in sync with the
  current manuscript but both are the rejected version.
