Dear Editors,

I am submitting "Free Speech as Causal Transparency" for consideration as an article in the
Journal of Ethics & Social Philosophy.

The paper proposes that an utterance is freely spoken when the causal pathway from the speaker's
communicative intentions to the utterance is recoverable by a competent interlocutor. For a specific utterance, its freedom is
scalar (inversely proportional to the cost of making the pathway transparent) and parametrised
over topic-context pairs rather than treated as a global binary. Consequences include a structural
(non-moral) exclusion of lying from free speech, a common analytical scale for coercion and
censorship, a tractable account of how suppression propagates across inferentially connected
topics, and an argument that hate speech is orthogonal to the freedom question. The account is
developed in relation to Shiffrin, the Gricean tradition, the Austin–Searle speech-act tradition
(Langton, Hornsby, McGowan), and Bejan.

The paper is approximately 9,500 words including notes and references, is not under consideration
elsewhere, and is prepared for anonymous review. I am an independent researcher with a background
in formal methods and computer science, writing on free speech from outside the discipline.

Thank you for your consideration.
